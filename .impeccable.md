# Backstop design direction

## Design Context

### Users

Backstop is for ordinary people facing a confusing medical insurance denial or
wrongful bill. They may be anxious, tired, short on time, and unfamiliar with
insurance language. Their job is to understand what happened, see trustworthy
evidence, approve only what they are comfortable sending, and follow the case
without learning a new system.

### Brand Personality

Calm, protective, exact.

The interface should lower the user's pulse while making every consequential
step legible. It should sound plainspoken and capable, never clinical,
patronizing, combative, or falsely reassuring.

The craft standard is Ryo Lu's: Stripe, Cursor, and Notion look easy to
recreate, and almost nobody comes close. Ease is the result of obsessive
detail — alignment, type, transitions, copy, focus, empty states, and the
demo sequence — not missing chrome.

### Aesthetic Direction

Build a product surface, not a magazine. Quiet canvas, precise type, generous
whitespace, one scarce accent, and document material (letters, clauses,
citations, audit) as the content. The demo video should feel pleasant because
every state change is composed, not because it is decorated.

References:

- Stripe, Cursor, Notion (Ryo Lu): deceptively simple product UI, tactile
  micro-detail, confident hierarchy, workflows that feel obvious
- Ramp: quiet white canvas, one high-clarity action, no dashboard clutter
- Interface Craft: uncommon care and anticipation
- User Interface Wiki: established interaction patterns
- Rams: measurable accessibility and anti-slop review
- Footer Design: intentional closing composition
- Recent: contemporary art direction without copying layouts

The product must not copy any reference. It must not resemble a generic medical
portal, SaaS dashboard, legal-tech template, or AI chatbot. Avoid glass,
gradients, glow, interchangeable cards, excessive pills, decorative charts,
stock hospital imagery, and novelty motion.

### Design Principles

1. **The document is the interface.** Evidence, quoted clauses, citations, and
   approval marks carry the visual identity.
2. **Calm comes from order.** Use alignment, whitespace, and hierarchy rather
   than hiding necessary detail.
3. **Consequences stay visible.** Draft, approved, and sent states must never be
   visually confusable.
4. **Editorial does not mean ornamental.** Every visual choice must improve
   comprehension, provenance, or confidence.
5. **Accessibility is craft.** Meet WCAG AA, preserve keyboard navigation,
   provide visible focus, use 44px targets, respect reduced motion, and adapt
   layouts rather than removing mobile functionality.
6. **The demo is a designed journey.** Loading, status transitions, approval,
   send confirmation, and the arriving reply must compose into one pleasant,
   legible three-minute sequence.

### System Rules

- Use OKLCH color tokens and tint neutrals toward the brand hue.
- Use a 4px spacing scale with named semantic tokens.
- Use a researched display/body type pairing; do not use common AI-default
  families.
- Use fixed product-interface type sizes with a fluid display scale only where
  editorial headlines genuinely need it.
- Use thin rules, tonal fields, and typography instead of rounded shadow cards.
- Use motion only for state transitions and live Convex updates, with
  exponential deceleration and a reduced-motion equivalent.
- Keep body copy between 65 and 75 characters per line.
- Prefer progressive disclosure over modals.
